The engagement detail screen is the control centre for one deal: its terms, product, costs, logistics and performance, plus the controls to advance the pipeline, add notes, and flag damage to customer support. This is where most of the work happens.

The layout

The header shows the engagement number, the influencer, the current stage and deal badges, a Complete or Incomplete pill once the deal is Live, and two buttons: Advance and Flag Damage to Pitstop. A metric with a reason recorded on the per-video Performance card counts toward Complete even without a number entered. A pipeline stepper shows progress. Below sit cards for Deal Terms, Product, Influencer, Payments, Costs (with the rolled-up total in orange), Ads, Logistics, Post-live and Performance (one tab per video, deal totals, sales per ₹1), followed by Notes and a History log. Notes show who wrote them and the History log shows who made each change, by name. Next to the pipeline stepper, a deal that has been approved reads “Approved {date} · by {name}”.

Complete is a derived flag, not a stage

Live is still the final stage — nothing about a deal moves when it becomes Complete. Complete is worked out, not clicked: a deal reads Complete once it is at stage Live and four things have all been answered — Views (greater than zero), Likes, Followers gained, and Cost (a total cost greater than zero). A number is one way to answer; a recorded reason is the other. If you note on the Performance card why a metric will never exist — Internal gap, Gated data or System timing — that metric counts as answered and the deal can be Complete without it. Cost is the exception: a reason never completes Cost, because an uncosted deal is a different problem from a number the platform will not show us. The pill sits next to the stage and deal-type badges in the header: a green Complete pill when all four are answered, or a muted Incomplete · missing: … pill naming exactly which are still blank with no reason given. Nothing shows before the deal reaches Live — an unposted deal has no numbers to be missing yet.

Entering the missing numbers in the Performance card flips the pill immediately, with no reload, because it is read straight from the same deal data the page already holds. Since 4 Sep 2026 a deal can carry more than one video: Views, Likes and Followers gained are now the sum across every take, so the pill reads the deal totals, not any one video's numbers.

ⓘ Why a flag, not a stage

Reann originally asked for a Complete stage. It was built as a derived flag instead so the stage vocabulary does not grow, and so nothing has to be clicked to mark a deal done — entering the numbers is marking it done.

ⓘ Historic Live deals were backfilled on 11 Sep 2026

Followers gained had never been entered on any deal, so every Live deal read Incomplete. On 11 Sep 2026 every already-live deal had Followers gained marked with the reason Internal gap, which answers the question without a number and lets those deals read Complete. New deals still need a real number or a declared reason as they go live.

Complete locks the deal terms

Since 11 Sep 2026, a deal that reads Complete also locks: Deal Terms, Costs, Post-live, Products and Compliance go read-only, each showing a small locked note in place of its Edit button. Everything else on the page stays open — Performance, payments, notes, stage, owner, logistics, codes and the Ads card can still be changed on a Complete deal. A lock banner appears in the header: “This deal is complete and locked. Metrics stay editable.”

There are two ways back to editable. The first is on you: clear a metric or its declared gap reason in Performance, which makes the deal Incomplete again and the lock falls away on its own. The second needs approval rights (the same people who can Approve a deal): press Unlock for edit in the header banner, optionally with a reason. That opens a 24-hour window, shown as an “Unlocked” banner naming who unlocked it and until when, and every unlock is logged. Lock again in that banner ends the window early. If nobody presses Lock again, the window simply expires on its own after 24 hours.

ⓘ Who can Unlock

Marketers without approval rights see “Unlocking is with Reann” in place of the button, the same pattern as Approve. Ask whoever holds approval rights to unlock the deal if a locked field genuinely needs fixing.

⚠ A live deal missing Cost or Views gets flagged

Once a deal is Live, a banner appears at the top of the page if Cost or Views is missing — the video is publicly up but its numbers were never entered, and without them CPM and cost-per-video cannot be worked out. It is a warning, not a block: the deal still saves and advances normally. Fill costs in the Costs card and views in the Performance card to clear it.

Deal Terms shows the Post date as “<date> · fixed, in the contract” or “· expected, not in the contract”. Press Edit to change the date, or to tick Fixed date (launch) — put this date in the contract; both can be changed after the deal is created (the tick is hidden on UGC deals). Changing either adds a Post date change line to the deal’s History, old → new. The date still drives the Schedule and the overdue flag whether fixed or not.

Deal Terms also records ad rights: whether the creator has agreed we may run the video as a paid ad, what we paid for that specifically, and how long we may run it. The amount is separate from the creator’s fee and counts toward the deal’s total cost. Ad rights shows a dash until someone answers the question — a dash means nobody has recorded it, which is not the same as No.

The Influencer card repeats who the deal is with — handle, name, phone, email, location, platform, type and follower count — so you can check you are looking at the right person without opening their profile. It is read-only; edit the creator on their own page.

The Payments card shows what has been paid against this deal and what is still owed, and every payment carries its screenshot: click it to open the proof. + Record logs a new payment with the creator and deal already filled in. The separate Payments page is still there for spend across all deals.

The Performance card: one tab per video

Since 4 Sep 2026 a deal can carry more than one video (Reann asked for 2 to 3 takes under one deal, so a second posting no longer has to pretend to be a separate deal). The card shows a row of tabs, Video #1, Video #2 and so on, one per take that has been added. Edit opens the take currently selected and its own metrics: link, posted date, views, paid views, likes, comments, shares, reposts, saves, followers gained, followers at post date, and (where the deal drives traffic) impressions. A blank metric can carry a “why blank?” reason instead of a number, for the ones that genuinely were not tracked. Save writes that one take only, nothing else on the deal. Posted date is required as soon as the take has views or paid views; Save is blocked until it is set.

+ Add video sits at the end of the tab row and adds another take, up to a limit of six videos per deal; the button disappears once a deal reaches six. Video #1 is the primary take: its link, posted date and followers-at-post-date are the deal’s own, the same numbers the Post-live card shows, and it cannot be removed while any other take exists. Every other tab, while editing, shows a Remove video #N button that deletes that take; its numbers leave the deal’s totals immediately.

ⓘ Since 11 Sep 2026: Views split into Paid and Organic

Views is the platform total for that take. Paid views is the slice an ad bought — type it, or leave it, and once an ad on that take is synced from Meta it fills in on its own. Underneath, the card shows Organic views: views minus paid, worked out for you, not a field you type into. Paid views can never be more than Views; Save is blocked until that is fixed. A take with no ad on it simply leaves Paid views blank, which reads as fully organic. CPM and every performance ratio use organic views, not the platform total — so a heavily-boosted video does not make its CPM look artificially cheap.

⚠ Followers at post date is required once any number is on the take

A take with only a link and a posted date can be saved with nothing else filled in. But the moment any number is entered on that take — views, likes, comments, shares, reposts, saves, impressions, followers gained or paid views — Followers at post date stops accepting a “why blank?” reason and Save stays disabled until it too has a number. It cannot be recovered later, so it has to be captured while you have it, or that take loses it for good. This applies per take, not once per deal.

Owner

The Owner card shows who on the influencer team is taking the deal forward (this used to be called the POC). With manage access you can change it from the drop-down; the change is logged in History. About 280 older deals have no owner yet: the card says so, and picking one there fills it in. The list is the Podium Influencer department, limited to people with Ignition access.

Deal totals: the rollup below the tabs

Deal totals sits under the tabs and shows the deal’s own numbers: views, likes, comments, shares, reposts, saves, followers gained and impressions are now the sum of every video take, and are no longer typed directly here. Edit totals only opens Sessions, Orders and Sales (coupon, net) ₹, which stay deal-level and are not attached to any one video. On a deal with an affiliate code, Orders and Sales fill in from the code sync. The card shows Sales per ₹1 (coupon net sales divided by spend) instead of ROAS. The engagement ratios and CPM are all recomputed from these totals, and since 11 Sep 2026 they are computed on organic views (views minus paid views), not the platform total.

ⓘ Video #1’s posted date IS the deal’s

Video #1’s posted date is the deal’s posted date — the Post-live card and the Video #1 tab show the same date, and editing it in either place updates both. The video link is edited on the Video #1 tab; the Post-live card only displays it. The Post-live card’s Tracking link is a different thing entirely (the deal’s UTM link for the store).

The Logistics card: hand-entered fields plus live courier status

The Logistics card starts with the fields someone typed in: Shipping order, Tracking, Shipping date and Delivered. Since 10 Sep 2026 the card also shows, alongside those, what the courier itself is reporting: a Courier row with a lifecycle badge (Pending, Manifested, In transit, Out for delivery, Delivered, RTO or Cancelled) and the courier’s name, an AWB row with the tracking number (a live link to Delhivery’s public tracking page for Delhivery parcels, plain text for everyone else, because there is no page to send other carriers to), and Dispatched / Delivered (courier) timestamps, in IST. This is all read straight from the courier feed; it never overwrites the hand-entered fields above it.

A small “matched by order prefix” note means the typed Shipping order had something extra on the end (for example #LOT43838 Complete) and the match was made on the order number inside it. The data itself is real; the note exists so nobody assumes it was an exact match.

Two states show as plain grey text rather than a badge: “Awaiting courier sync” means there is an order number but the courier feed has not reached Ignition yet, which is normal for a deal created in the last few days. “Sent via Porter — no tracking” (or DTDC, or any other name) means the Shipping order field holds a courier name rather than an order number, because the parcel was hand-delivered or sent by a courier outside the feed. Neither is an error and neither needs fixing.

⚠ Stage and courier status are different things, and will often disagree

The deal’s stage tracks the content deal (Shipped, Live, Posting and so on). The courier’s lifecycle badge tracks the parcel. A deal at Live with a parcel reading Delivered is completely normal — the video went up and the box also arrived. Never change a deal’s stage just to make it match the courier. An RTO means the parcel came back to us and the creator never received it at all; that is a real problem to decide on, not something to fix by clicking a stage.

The tracking link

Every deal can carry its own tracking link — a short lottoys.in address that sends the customer to Legend of Toys and records that the visit came from this creator and this deal. It appears in the Post-live card, in full, with a copy button, because the whole point is to hand the string to a creator.

A deal gets one automatically when it reaches Shipped. For older deals, or if one is missing, click Create tracking link. The link points at the product page whenever we can work out which product the deal is for, and at the shop otherwise. Picking the product from the dropdown when you create the deal is what makes that certain; if it was typed by hand we still match it to the catalogue where the name is unambiguous. Where two different products could be meant, the link deliberately goes to the shop rather than risk sending a creator’s followers to the wrong page.

⚠ Give the creator this link, not the shop address

A plain link to the website cannot be told apart from anyone else’s traffic. Only the tracking link credits the visits, and any resulting orders, to the creator who sent them. Once a creator has posted it, it must not be changed — it is already out in the world on their page.

The link opens the deal’s own colour

The link lands on the exact colour saved on the deal, not just the product page. This matters because a product’s colours all share one page on the website, so a link that only names the product opens whichever colour the shop lists first — a Tarmac Purple deal would have shown Tarmac Black.

Set the product before you create the link. If the product on a deal is changed after the link exists, the link keeps pointing where it was first sent, and Ignition cannot move it — the Create tracking link button only appears while a deal has no link. The link itself stays valid, so anything already shared keeps working.

⚠ A stale link now warns you, on its own

If the tracking link was minted and the deal’s product changed afterward, a banner appears right under the link showing where it currently points and where the deal now points. Ignition still cannot repoint it: every target change is recorded against a named person, so fixing it is done in Relay → Links, not here. The banner links straight there.

The Ads card: boosting a video as a paid ad

Since 11 Sep 2026 the Ads card, below Costs, tracks running a deal’s video as a paid Meta ad — separate from the organic post itself. Each row is one ad and boosts one take of the deal’s video (pick it from the Take dropdown), plus Platform (Instagram, Facebook, or both), start and end dates, an optional Meta ad id and a note. + Add ad starts a new one; Edit and Delete manage an existing one.

Two separate things are tracked on every ad, and they do not always agree:

  • Approval — Legend of Toys’s go-ahead to run it. Only people with approval rights (the same rights that approve a deal) see Approve, Reject and Back to pending. Approve stays greyed out, with the date shown, until 10 days after that take posted (in IST) — there is nothing to click early, and the card tells you exactly which date it opens.
  • Run status — what the ad is actually doing: Not started, Running or Ended. You cannot hand-set an ad to Running until it is approved; the option is disabled and marked “(needs approval)” until then. Meta’s own reporting can still show an ad running that was never approved here — that shows as a red “running without approval” flag on the row, a warning to go sort out, not something Ignition blocks.

Refresh from Meta (once any ad on the deal has a Meta ad id) pulls status, spend, views and impressions from Meta for every ad on the deal and shows when each last synced; a sync older than 36 hours is flagged as stale, meaning the daily automatic sync has stopped reaching it.

⚠ Ad money is outside the influencer budget

What is paid here, and any Meta spend, is never part of this deal’s total cost, its CPM, the monthly budget or the Payments page. The Ads card totals its own spend separately at the top of the card. The Costs card no longer has an Ad spend field to enter — that input was retired on 11 Sep 2026; ad money now lives only here.

Ad payments, underneath the ads list, is money paid to the creator for ad rights or usage — a different thing from Meta spend, which is what Legend of Toys pays Meta to run the ad. + Record logs a payment: amount, Pending or Paid, optionally linked to one ad, with a note. Marking one Paid requires a screenshot of the payment, the same rule as the deal’s own Payments card; click screenshot on any row to view it. Back to pending and delete are also available.

ⓘ The Ads card is never locked

Unlike Deal Terms, Costs, Post-live, Products and Compliance, the Ads card and its payments stay editable even on a Complete, locked deal — ads typically run after the video is already up and the deal has completed.

Deliverables and the contract

Deal Terms has a Deliverables field: what the creator will make, in plain words (for example “1 reel, 3 stories”). It is also on the New deal form. The contract email quotes it, and a contract cannot be sent without it. Sending the contract, and the Shipping lock that goes with it, are covered in The Deal Contract. The Send contract window says which template it uses (Barter, Paid or Affiliate), picked from the deal type, and will not send an affiliate deal without its commission % or any deal without a deal type.

TDS banner on products

If the creator is getting close to the TDS limit on products this financial year, the deal page shows the same banner as the creator page: amber from ₹15,000 (“Close to the ₹20,000 TDS limit”), red above ₹20,000 (“Over ₹20,000 in products this year: TDS applies…”; TDS under section 194R, check with accounts before sending more). Under ₹15,000 nothing shows. If a product line has no price on file, the banner says the real figure may be higher. Partners never see it. The full running figure is on the creator page, under Influencers.

Approving a proposed deal

A deal sitting in Proposed cannot move on until it is approved. Since 26 Aug 2026 that is the Marketing head’s decision alone; everyone else sees “Approval is with Reann” where the button used to be, and keeps every other control. A deal you are turning down can still be dropped or ghosted without approving it first.

Approving now moves the deal for you. Since 27 Aug 2026 a deal you approve goes straight to Planning (a UGC deal goes to Outreach) — you no longer approve it and then advance it as two separate jobs.

Advancing the deal

Click Advance to open the Move to… window. It works in up to two steps:

  1. Pick the stage. A grid shows every stage you can move to; the next stage on the happy path is marked NEXT. Choose one and add an optional note. If you are skipping stages it warns you but lets you continue. A stage that needs nothing extra has an Advance button right here; otherwise the button reads Continue.
  2. Fill in what that stage needs. Only the fields the deal does not already hold are asked for. Live asks for the video link, the video posting date and, if the influencer is unrated, a Green, Yellow or Red rating. Shipped asks for the Shopify order id (a UGC deal asks for a tracking link instead). Scheduled asks Is it on track?: On track, or Delayed with a revised post date, which moves the deal to the Delayed stage and keeps the original date in the history. ← Back returns to the grid.

The happy path is Proposed, Planning, Shipped, Delivered, Draft received, Scheduled, Live, and Live is the finish line — the video is up, the deal is done. A deal that falls through exits to Ghosted, Dropped or Cancelled (use Cancelled when the deal was called off before anything was spent — it keeps the record but stays out of every spend and CPM total); On hold and Delayed hold a deal that has stalled without dying. You may move to any stage in any order — the one thing you cannot do is leave Proposed without an approval.

ⓘ “Agreed” is gone

The old Agreed stage was retired on 27 Aug 2026: approving a deal is now the go-ahead, so the stage was saying the same thing twice. Deals that were sitting on it moved to Planning. Old history entries still read “Agreed” — that is the record of what happened at the time, not an error.

ⓘ You can skip stages — on purpose

Real deals do not queue politely, so Advance lets you move to any stage in any order. If you skip past a stage on the happy path it simply warns you and lets you continue. Terminal stages are not dead ends either: reopening a ghosted or cancelled deal clears its closing date and puts it back in play. Notes you add and every stage move are recorded in the History log.

⛔ Flagging damage opens a real support ticket

Flag Damage to Pitstop opens a genuine ticket in the Pitstop customer-support system, prefilled with this deal’s influencer info, with a disposition (replacement, refund or repair) and a required description. This is a deliberate manual handoff, used when a product sent to an influencer arrives damaged. Only one Pitstop ticket can be opened per engagement; once raised, its number shows in the Logistics card.

The Commission card (affiliate deals)

On Affiliate and Paid + Affiliate deals, a Commission card lists each month: the number of orders, net sales, the Ignition estimate, the D2C verified amount (with who verified it and when), the Reason if it differs, and the payment status. The current month shows “so far”. Months appear once the code has sales in them; before that the card says there are no commission months yet. The code’s “commission” figure elsewhere is a running total; the month D2C verifies is what gets paid. Verifying happens on Analyze → Commission, not here.

The same rule applies when you edit a deal’s terms: Affiliate % is required on Affiliate and Paid + Affiliate deals, and a save without it shows “An affiliate deal needs its commission % (more than 0, at most 100).” The same goes for the New deal window.
