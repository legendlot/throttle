The engagement detail screen is the control centre for one deal: its terms, product, costs, logistics and performance, plus the controls to advance the pipeline, add notes, and flag damage to customer support. This is where most of the work happens.

The layout

The header shows the engagement number, the influencer, the current stage and deal badges, and two buttons: Advance and Flag Damage to Pitstop. A pipeline stepper shows progress. Below sit cards for Deal Terms, Product, Influencer, Payments, Costs (with the rolled-up total in orange), Logistics, Post-live and Performance (views, orders, ROAS), followed by Notes and a History log.

Deal Terms also records ad rights: whether the creator has agreed we may run the video as a paid ad, what we paid for that specifically, and how long we may run it. The amount is separate from the creator’s fee and counts toward the deal’s total cost. Ad rights shows a dash until someone answers the question — a dash means nobody has recorded it, which is not the same as No.

The Influencer card repeats who the deal is with — handle, name, phone, email, location, platform, type and follower count — so you can check you are looking at the right person without opening their profile. It is read-only; edit the creator on their own page.

The Payments card shows what has been paid against this deal and what is still owed, and every payment carries its screenshot: click it to open the proof. + Record logs a new payment with the creator and deal already filled in. The separate Payments page is still there for spend across all deals.

The tracking link

Every deal can carry its own tracking link — a short lottoys.in address that sends the customer to Legend of Toys and records that the visit came from this creator and this deal. It appears in the Post-live card, in full, with a copy button, because the whole point is to hand the string to a creator.

A deal gets one automatically when it reaches Shipped. For older deals, or if one is missing, click Create tracking link. The link points at the product page where we know it, and at the shop otherwise.

⚠ Give the creator this link, not the shop address

A plain link to the website cannot be told apart from anyone else’s traffic. Only the tracking link credits the visits, and any resulting orders, to the creator who sent them. Once a creator has posted it, it must not be changed — it is already out in the world on their page.

The deal brief

Preview brief (in the header) writes a draft of the collaboration email for this deal: what we are sending, the posting date, the tracking link, the discount code, and what we would like the creator to include. It also warns you up front if the influencer has no email address on record, or the deal has no tracking link, code or expected post date.

ⓘ It is a draft, and nothing sends it

Ignition cannot email creators. The brief is there to be read, argued with and copied out — use Copy brief and send it yourself. The wording is a starting point and is expected to change.

Approving a proposed deal

A deal sitting in Proposed cannot move on until it is approved. Since 26 Aug 2026 that is the Marketing head’s decision alone; everyone else sees “Approval is with Reann” where the button used to be, and keeps every other control. A deal you are turning down can still be dropped or ghosted without approving it first.

Approving now moves the deal for you. Since 27 Aug 2026 a deal you approve goes straight to Planning (a UGC deal goes to Outreach) — you no longer approve it and then advance it as two separate jobs.

Advancing the deal

Click Advance to move the deal to another stage, with an optional note. The happy path is Proposed, Planning, Shipped, Delivered, Draft received, Scheduled, Live, and Live is the finish line — the video is up, the deal is done. A deal that falls through exits to Ghosted, Dropped or Cancelled (use Cancelled when the deal was called off before anything was spent — it keeps the record but stays out of every spend and CPM total); On hold and Delayed hold a deal that has stalled without dying. You may move to any stage in any order — the one thing you cannot do is leave Proposed without an approval.

ⓘ “Agreed” is gone

The old Agreed stage was retired on 27 Aug 2026: approving a deal is now the go-ahead, so the stage was saying the same thing twice. Deals that were sitting on it moved to Planning. Old history entries still read “Agreed” — that is the record of what happened at the time, not an error.

ⓘ You can skip stages — on purpose

Real deals do not queue politely, so Advance lets you move to any stage in any order. If you skip past a stage on the happy path it simply warns you and lets you continue. Terminal stages are not dead ends either: reopening a ghosted or cancelled deal clears its closing date and puts it back in play. Notes you add and every stage move are recorded in the History log.

⛔ Flagging damage opens a real support ticket

Flag Damage to Pitstop opens a genuine ticket in the Pitstop customer-support system, prefilled with this deal’s influencer info, with a disposition (replacement, refund or repair) and a required description. This is a deliberate manual handoff, used when a product sent to an influencer arrives damaged. Only one Pitstop ticket can be opened per engagement; once raised, its number shows in the Logistics card.
