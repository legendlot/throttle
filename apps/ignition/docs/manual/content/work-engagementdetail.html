The engagement detail screen is the control centre for one deal: its terms, product, costs, logistics and performance, plus the controls to advance the pipeline, add notes, and flag damage to customer support. This is where most of the work happens.

What it’s for
Running one deal end to end.

Pipeline
13 stages, 3 failure exits.

The layout

The header shows the engagement number, the influencer, the current stage and deal badges, and two buttons: Advance and Flag Damage to Pitstop. A pipeline stepper shows progress. Below sit cards for Deal Terms, Product, Costs (with the rolled-up total in orange), Logistics, Post-live and Performance (views, orders, ROAS), followed by Notes and a History log.

The tracking link

Every deal can carry its own tracking link — a short lottoys.in address that sends the customer to Legend of Toys and records that the visit came from this creator and this deal. It appears in the Post-live card, in full, with a copy button, because the whole point is to hand the string to a creator.

A deal gets one automatically when it reaches Shipped. For older deals, or if one is missing, click Create tracking link. The link points at the product page where we know it, and at the shop otherwise.

⚠ Give the creator this link, not the shop address

A plain link to the website cannot be told apart from anyone else’s traffic. Only the tracking link credits the visits, and any resulting orders, to the creator who sent them. Once a creator has posted it, it must not be changed — it is already out in the world on their page.

The deal brief

Preview brief (in the header) writes a draft of the collaboration email for this deal: what we are sending, the posting date, the tracking link, the discount code, and what we would like the creator to include. It also warns you up front if the influencer has no email address on record, or the deal has no tracking link, code or expected post date.

ⓘ It is a draft, and nothing sends it

Ignition cannot email creators. The brief is there to be read, argued with and copied out — use Copy brief and send it yourself. The wording is a starting point and is expected to change.

Approving a proposed deal

A deal sitting in Proposed cannot move on until it is approved. Since 26 Aug 2026 that is the Marketing head’s decision alone; everyone else sees “Approval is with Reann” where the button used to be, and keeps every other control. A deal you are turning down can still be dropped or ghosted without approving it first.

Advancing the deal

Click Advance to move the deal to its next stage. A modal offers only the stages you are allowed to move to from where you are, plus an optional note. The happy path is Identified, Invited, Engaged, Negotiating, Agreed, Shipped, Delivered, Script Review, Script Signed Off, Scheduled, Live, Tracking, Closed; a deal that falls through exits to Declined, Ghosted or Dropped (from which the only move is to Closed).

ⓘ You cannot skip stages

Advance only ever offers the permitted next stages, so a deal moves forward one controlled step at a time and never jumps ahead or back. Once a deal is Closed the Advance button is disabled: it is a dead end. Notes you add and every stage move are recorded in the History log.

⛔ Flagging damage opens a real support ticket

Flag Damage to Pitstop opens a genuine ticket in the Pitstop customer-support system, prefilled with this deal’s influencer info, with a disposition (replacement, refund or repair) and a required description. This is a deliberate manual handoff, used when a product sent to an influencer arrives damaged. Only one Pitstop ticket can be opened per engagement; once raised, its number shows in the Logistics card.
