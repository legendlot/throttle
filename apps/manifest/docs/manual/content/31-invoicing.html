Invoicing is how SF closes an order out, with a formal SF to LOT invoice for LOT’s books and GST. It is a record, not the thing that moves the money: goods and logistics already left the pool as they were paid.

Raising an invoice

  1. Open Invoice & close. On a delivered (or partly invoiced) order, the Invoice card lists the goods lines.
  2. Tick the lines to bill. Partial invoicing is allowed: bill some now and the rest later. Already-billed lines show their invoice number and cannot be billed twice.
  3. Set GST per line. Each line carries its own GST percent (defaults to 18). A single PO can mix items on different GST codes, so set each correctly.
  4. Optionally add commission. Toggle the SF 2.5% commission on or off for the invoice.
  5. Submit. Manifest assigns the invoice number automatically (the VWINV series), stamps each billed line, and updates the order’s state.

How the invoice is valued

Priced lines

A line with a per-unit Yuan price is valued as price times quantity times the exchange rate.

Lump-cost lines

Some items (moulds, machines, mixed sets) have no per-unit price. Those bill the order’s recorded rupee cost instead, so the order is still closeable.

Invoice number

Auto-assigned from the VWINV series; never typed by hand. The financial year rolls over automatically each April.

What invoicing moves

⛔ Important

The only thing invoicing debits from the pool is the 2.5% commission (if you include it). Goods and logistics already left the pool when they were paid. The GST on the invoice is a document and compliance figure; it is not a separate pool movement.

ⓘ Note

The order becomes Partially invoiced while some goods lines are still unbilled, and Invoiced (closed) once they all carry an invoice number.
