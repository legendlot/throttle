An order in Manifest flows through one continuous, party-owned lifecycle. LOT starts it with a request; Solve Factory drives it the rest of the way to a closed invoice. This chapter is the map; the next chapters are the steps.

The journey, end to end

  1. LOT requests it. The order is Requested. This is the only thing LOT does to the order itself.
  2. SF converts it to a PO. SF fills the vendor, the vendor’s product codes and the prices in Yuan. The order becomes Draft.
  3. SF places the PO. The order becomes Placed and a printable PO document unlocks. SF sends it to the vendor.
  4. Vendor sends the PI; SF attaches it. Stored as evidence and stamped on the timeline.
  5. SF pays the advance. Deducts the pool.
  6. Production to pickup. Confirmed, Produced, Picked up. At pickup SF pays any balance. Goods purchase is now wrapped up.
  7. SF ships it. Onto a shipment (air or sea), then sailing, docking, customs, last-mile. Costs paid along the way.
  8. Delivered. The goods reach the warehouse.
  9. SF invoices LOT. Partially invoiced, then Invoiced once everything is billed.

The states an order moves through

Lifecycle

Requested → Draft → Placed → Confirmed → Produced → Picked up → Shipped → Received → Delivered, plus Cancelled.

Billing

Runs in parallel near the end: In flight → Delivered → Partially invoiced → Invoiced.

The short version (cheat sheet)

  1. LOT requests, LOT funds the pool. Everything else is SF.
  2. One shared pool. Every SF payment deducts it the moment it’s recorded.
  3. SF flow. Convert, place (PO PDF), attach PI, pay advance, produce, pickup (pay balance), ship, clear customs (pay shipping/customs/fees), deliver (pay last-mile), invoice.
  4. Cancellable until pickup, never after.
  5. Invoice is a close-out record. Partial allowed; GST editable per line; optional 2.5% commission.
⛔ Important

An order can be cancelled at any time up to and including Produced. Once it is Picked up, it can no longer be cancelled, because the goods are paid for and on their way.
