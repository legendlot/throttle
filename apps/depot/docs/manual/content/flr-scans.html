The Scan Feed is the live stream of every dispatch scan as it happens on the floor. Open it when you want to see what just moved, confirm a unit was scanned at the right step, or trace one box by its UPC. It is read-only; nothing here changes a record.

Watching the dispatch scan stream live.

Read-only.

Scope
Dispatch scan activities only.

What it shows

This is the dispatch slice of the wider scan log: it lists only the scan steps that belong to dispatch, so it stays uncluttered by production scans. Those steps are returned-to-ecom (RTE), returned-to-retail (RTR), DTK, Allocate, Pack, Dispatch out (DOut), returns in (RTO In), RTD Return, and the two repack steps (Repack In and Repack Out).

The table reads newest first. Each row shows the time, the unit’s UPC, the activity, the line (D1 or D2), the product, the operator who scanned it, the loop count, and whether the scan is OK or has been voided.

Reading and filtering

Date and presets

Pick a single day, or use Today, This Week, This Month. Choosing a range shows a From and To field.

Summary tiles

The cards across the top count the day’s scans per activity (Total, RTE, RTR, DTK, Allocated, Packed, Dispatched, Returns In, RTD Return, Voided). Click a tile to filter the table to that activity; click Total to clear.

Activity chips

The row of chips does the same filtering as the tiles, and also covers the Repack In / Repack Out steps that are kept off the headline tiles.

Search UPC / box label / AWB

Type at least three characters to switch into search mode: the table then shows the matching unit’s full dispatch history across time, not just the chosen day. The same box now takes a UPC, a box label, or a courier AWB, and works both ways: search an AWB to see the unit(s) shipped on it, or search a unit’s UPC to see the AWB it went out on. Clear the box to return to the live feed.

AWB column

Searching adds an AWB column to the table, between UPC and Activity, shown only in search mode. The normal date-filtered feed keeps its original columns; AWB never appears there.

Show Voided

Off by default. Tick it to include scans that were later voided (they appear dimmed and tagged Voided).

Three things to know about AWB search

One AWB, many units

Bulk cartons ship 32 or 64 cars on a single AWB. Searching that AWB can legitimately return every unit in the carton at once, that is correct, not a duplicate.

AWBs only from 9 Sep 2026

AWBs are captured at the Pack station, and only from 9 September 2026 onwards. Any box packed before that has no AWB on record: the column reads “not captured”, which is expected, not missing data. As of 10 September 2026 only around 400 of roughly 87,000 units have one recorded, and the count grows with every box packed from here.

Letters-only labels can’t be searched

Courier labels that contain letters, such as Shadowfax’s SF…, self-ship 6679_SP/26-…, or some Shiprocket SRSC… labels, are never recorded at all, so they cannot be searched. If an all-numbers AWB does not come up, that is worth reporting as a bug; a letters-containing one not coming up is expected and is a known open issue.

✓ Tip

Use the search box to settle a “where did this box go?” question either direction: search a unit’s UPC to see the AWB it shipped on, or search an AWB to see everything that went out on it, in order, with the operator and time on each scan. If nothing comes back, the screen tells you which of three things happened: no unit is linked to that AWB (and reminds you AWBs start from 9 Sep 2026), units were found but your activity or voided filters are hiding them, or units were found but none has reached a dispatch station yet. The list loads in pages; click Load More at the bottom to pull older scans for the selected day.
