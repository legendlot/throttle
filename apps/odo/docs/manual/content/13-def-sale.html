"A sale" does not mean the same event on every channel, because LOT does not have the same commercial relationship with every channel. This chapter says what Odo counts, channel by channel.

Default
The consumer’s purchase.
Exception
Outright buyers: our sale to them.
Grain
Date × channel × product.
Day
IST, not UTC.

Two kinds of channel

Everything turns on who owns the stock when the consumer buys it.

We sell to the consumer

Website, Amazon, and the quick-commerce platforms. The stock is ours until a shopper buys it, so the consumer purchase is the sale and that is what Odo counts.

They buy from us outright

Flipkart Managed, General Trade, Modern Trade, Peeko. They purchase stock on a PO and then sell it on their own account. Our sale to them is the sale, and Odo counts the confirmed sales order.

⚠ Watch out

For an outright buyer, Odo shows nothing about what they subsequently sold to shoppers. That is not a gap in the data; we genuinely do not receive it and it is not ours to count. If someone asks "how is Flipkart selling through?", Odo cannot answer that question, and no screen in it should be read as if it can.

Flipkart changed, and it was not a broken feed

Flipkart used to be a marketplace where LOT was the seller, so consumer orders flowed to us. They now buy outright. Consumer sales therefore stopped reaching our systems in June by design, and Odo's Flipkart number switched to the purchase order. Anyone looking at a long Flipkart trend will see the basis change mid-year. It is a commercial change, not a data one.

ⓘ Note

The day boundary is IST. A sale placed at 11pm on the 3rd belongs to the 3rd, not the 4th. Channel portals often report in their own timezone, which is a common source of small day-level differences at month ends.
