Odo is the one place where every LOT sale is counted, whatever channel it came from. It pulls Website, Amazon, Flipkart, quick-commerce, General and Modern Trade and the long-tail channels into a single set of numbers, so a question like "what did we sell last month?" has one answer instead of nine.

Why this manual leads with definitions

Most disagreements about an Odo number are not errors. They are two people using the same word to mean different things.

The clearest example, and the reason this manual exists: when the Channels team says gross, they usually mean revenue with returns, refunds and taxes already taken out. When Odo says gross, it means the opposite end of the ladder: the full order value before discounts, with tax still inside it. Both are reasonable. Neither is wrong. But if one person quotes Odo's gross into a conversation built on the other definition, the two figures will differ by a wide margin and everybody will start hunting for a bug that is not there.

So the second part of this manual, The Definitions, is the part to read first. Every other chapter assumes you have.

✓ Tip

If a number in Odo disagrees with a number in a channel's own portal, check the definition before checking the feed. In practice that is where the difference usually lives.

What Odo is not

Not an order manager

You cannot pick, pack, cancel or refund anything here. Odo reads what other systems did. Dispatch happens in Depot, procurement in Snorkel, customer conversations in Pitstop.

Not the invoice

Odo is a management view. The GST invoice and the filed return are produced elsewhere and are the authority for tax.

Not real time, except where it says so

Almost everything refreshes hourly. Only the Live screen and Website stock are faster. A figure for the last hour is normal to see move.

Where the numbers come from

Each channel has a connector that fetches its sales on a schedule and stages them. A shared calculation then turns those staged rows into the facts every screen reads. Two things follow from that design and are worth knowing early:

  • Re-pulling the same period is safe. The calculation replaces a day rather than adding to it, so a feed that runs twice does not double a number.
  • A correction lands on the original date. If an order placed on the 3rd is cancelled on the 9th, the 3rd changes. Yesterday's report can legitimately look different today.
⚠ Watch out

Because corrections back-date, a figure you screenshotted last week may not match the same figure today, and both can be correct. When you publish a number, publish the date you pulled it.
