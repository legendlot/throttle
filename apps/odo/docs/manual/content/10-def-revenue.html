This is the chapter that settles most arguments. Odo and the Channels team both say "gross" and mean opposite ends of the same ladder. Neither is wrong; they are different rungs.

Odo’s headline
Net revenue, ex-GST.
Odo’s gross
Before discounts, tax still inside.
Channels’ gross
Usually after returns and tax.
Rule
Name the rung, not just the word.

The ladder, top to bottom

Every revenue figure in Odo is one of these rungs. Read down the list and stop where your question is:

RungWhat it is
GrossThe full order value before discounts, with GST still inside it. This is the widest number Odo holds.
− DiscountsCoupon codes, promotions and order-level discounts.
− CancellationsThe whole order removed, dated to when it was placed.
− ReturnsRefunds and RTOs, dated to when they came back.
− GSTTax stripped out, so what is left is the taxable base.
= Net revenueThe headline. What LOT actually earned, excluding tax.
⛔ Important

Net is the metric. Gross exists so channels can be compared on the same basis and so the ladder has a starting point. The business does not work off gross, and a gross figure quoted as "revenue" will overstate what was earned.

Why Odo’s gross includes tax

Channels report differently: some quote prices with tax inside, some without. If Odo copied each channel's own convention, the channels could not be added together or compared. So every channel is normalised to the same tax-inclusive basis on the way in, the real tax is captured separately, and tax is stripped once at the end. That is why the top of the ladder looks larger than you might expect, and why the bottom is trustworthy.

Translating between Odo and a channel report

When someone hands you a figure, ask which of these it is before comparing:

"Gross" from a channel

Usually already net of returns and tax. That is close to Odo's net, not Odo's gross. Compare it to net.

"Total sales" from a portal

Almost always tax-inclusive and before returns. Closest to Odo's gross, though timing will still differ.

"Settlement" or "payout"

Neither rung. That is money received after the platform took its fees, and Odo does not treat it as revenue.

✓ Tip

A reconciliation that is out by roughly the tax rate is a definition mismatch, not a data problem. A reconciliation out by a few percent is usually timing. A reconciliation out by a lot is worth reporting.

One known simplification

The tax strip currently uses a single 18% rate. That is correct for the overwhelming majority of LOT revenue, but the L.O.T Build range is taxed at 5%, so its net is understated slightly. The amount is small and known. If you are reporting on L.O.T Build specifically rather than on the business as a whole, say that the net is conservative.
