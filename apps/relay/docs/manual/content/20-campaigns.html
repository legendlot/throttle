A campaign is a one-off broadcast: you pick an audience, pick a message, and send it. This is the busiest screen in Relay and the one where mistakes are most expensive, so it has the most guard rails.

What it’s for
Sending one message to one segment, once.
Read or act?
Action screen. Sends real messages.
Who
Author builds, Manager sends, Approver signs off.
Undo?
No. A started broadcast can be cancelled, not recalled.

The lifecycle

A campaign moves through fixed states, and what you can change depends on where it is.

⚠ Watch out

A campaign becomes read-only the moment you submit it. That is deliberate: an approver must sign off the thing that actually sends, not a version that changed afterwards. If you need a change, have it rejected back to draft.

Building one

  1. Name it. Use something you will recognise in Analytics in three months. “Diwali 2026 catalogue”, not “test 4”.
  2. Pick the channel and purpose. Purpose matters: marketing is gated on consent and quiet hours, transactional is not. Do not label a promotion as transactional to dodge the gate.
  3. Pick the audience. Choose a segment. The line underneath immediately tells you how many people will actually receive it.
  4. Set exclusions if you need them. See the Audience Exclusions chapter.
  5. Pick the template. Only templates for the chosen channel appear. On WhatsApp, only templates approved by Meta can be used.
  6. Send a test. The Send test button delivers the real message to a few named addresses, through the real gate, without touching the segment. Do this every time.
  7. Submit. Below the approval threshold it approves itself. Above it, it goes to an Approver.
  8. Send now, or schedule. Both need Manager. A confirmation dialog states exactly who is about to be messaged and which exclusions are in force.

Reading the audience line

Under the segment picker you will see something like:

24,929 will receive · 25,100 in segment · 33 suppressed/opted-out · 138 excluded by your rules

in segment

Everyone the segment rule matches, before any filtering.

suppressed/opted-out

Dropped because they have a hard block, or have not opted in to marketing on this channel.

excluded by your rules

Dropped by the exclusions you set on this campaign.

will receive

The number that matters. This is what the approval threshold is judged on, and what the confirmation dialog repeats back to you.

Send test

A test send uses the campaign’s real template and goes through the real send gate, so what lands on your phone is what a customer would get. It is recorded separately and never appears in the campaign’s own numbers. You can run it as many times as you like.

⛔ Important

When test mode is off, Send now reaches real customers immediately. The confirmation dialog will say so in as many words. Read it rather than clicking through it: it names the audience and lists the exclusions, and it is the last point at which stopping is free.

What happens after you press Send

Sending is no longer a single leap. The moment you press Send now (or the schedule fires), the campaign first takes a snapshot of exactly who it intends to message — the roster — and only then begins delivering, in several parallel lanes. Two consequences worth knowing:

Preparing the audience

For a short while the campaign shows this instead of sending. It is normal — a big audience takes a minute or two to snapshot — and sending starts by itself when it finishes. Stop works during this phase too.

N never attempted

After the campaign finishes, this badge is an exact count of people who were on the roster and never got any attempt — not sent, not failed, not skipped. One press of Send to the missed reaches exactly them; everyone already handled is skipped automatically.

Add people who became eligible since

New opt-ins keep arriving after the snapshot. This button tells you how many, and adds them to the roster — nothing sends until you press Resume.

Stalled

If a delivery lane fails repeatedly, the campaign parks itself here and posts an alert, instead of silently looking busy forever. Resume continues exactly where it stopped.

ℹ Why the numbers can differ from the audience line

The audience line is live and keeps moving as people opt in and out. The roster is frozen at the moment you sent. “Who did we miss?” is always answered against the roster, so the answer stays the same no matter when you ask.
