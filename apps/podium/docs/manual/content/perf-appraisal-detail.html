This is the single appraisal screen everyone uses, but it shows you a different face depending on who you are to it: the person being appraised, their manager, or HR. You reach it from the link on My Performance (for your own or one you owe) or from the cycle grid (for HR). It shows you exactly the part you need to fill in, and hides the rest until it's meant to be seen.

What it’s for
One person's appraisal, end to end.
Read or act?
Act: write, rate, calibrate, acknowledge.
Who
Employee Manager HR Admin
Scale
1–5 plus three written prompts.

The flow, in order

An appraisal moves through clear stages, each unlocking the next: self-review → manager review → HR calibration → shared with you → acknowledged. You only ever act on the stage that's yours.

If it's your appraisal Employee

  1. Give yourself an overall rating from 1 to 5, using the rating buttons.
  2. Answer the three prompts in writing: what went well, what could have gone better, and your focus for the next period.
  3. Rate your KPIs if your role has them (optional).
  4. Submit. You can keep editing while the cycle is open; submitting also lets your manager start their review.

Once HR shares the result, this screen shows Your result: your final rating, your manager's written feedback, and any increment. You can optionally acknowledge it with a note, and open a printable letter.

If you're the manager Manager

You see the person's submitted self-review first, then your own form: an overall rating, the same three prompts, and optional per-KPI ratings. Submit when ready; like the self-review, it stays editable while the cycle is open. You do not set the final rating, that's HR's calibration step, and you won't see the final number until it's finalized.

ⓘ The three prompts

Every review, self and manager, answers the same three questions: what went well, what could have gone better, and the focus for the next period. Keeping the prompts identical on both sides makes the two views easy to compare side by side at calibration.

If you're HR HR

You see both reviews in full, then the calibration panel:

  1. Set the final rating (1–5) and an optional calibration note. The note is HR-internal and never shown to the employee. Click Finalize.
  2. Record the increment if you have compensation access: an increment percentage and/or a one-time bonus. The effective date defaults to the cycle's anchor, and the suggested percentage comes from the rating band.
  3. Share with the employee. Until you do, they see nothing.
  4. Print the letters. An appraisal letter, and an increment letter where there's a revision, open as clean printable pages you can save as PDF.
⛔ The calibration note stays with HR

The calibration note is for HR's own record only. It is never shown to the employee or the manager, even after sharing. Use it for context behind a decision; never put anything in it you'd be uncomfortable keeping internal.

ⓘ Increments while the vault is off

With the salary vault disabled (the current default), an increment records the percentage and any bonus amount, not an absolute salary. The increment letter states the revision in those terms. Absolute figures only come into play once the vault is switched on, after the extra security layer is live (see Settings).
