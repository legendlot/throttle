Reports is the “how are we doing” screen: how many cases and calls happened over a date range, what they cost, and how products, platforms, agents and departments compare. Open it to review performance.

What it’s for
Ticket and call analytics.
Read or act?
Read; CSV export.
Who
Lead Admin
Range
From / To (defaults year-to-date).

Three tabs

Tickets shows total cases and the three cost lines (return logistics, replacement units, refund payouts), a monthly trend chart coloured by outcome, and breakdowns by product, platform and agent (with each agent’s average days-to-close). Calls shows total, answered and missed calls, answer rate and average duration, with breakdowns by department, account, direction and agent, plus an hourly distribution. Agents is the conversation report: per-agent queries, replies, closures and response times across every channel.

On the Agents tab, hover any column heading (dotted underline) or summary card for its exact definition — the wording was agreed with the CS team, so what you read is what the number means.

The Agents tab has a Business hours only tickbox, and it is on by default: response and resolution clocks run only during each agent’s own rostered shift, so overnight and days off are not counted against anyone. Untick it for the around-the-clock view — the same figures with the clock never stopping — which is the customer’s experience of the wait rather than the agent’s. The basis in use is named under each time card and in the CSV export.

⚠ Resolution changed meaning on 24 August 2026

Resolution is now Resolved ÷ Queries: conversations genuinely resolved, as a share of queries raised. It previously counted every closure — including spam and duplicates — which overstated the rate by roughly three times. If you compare against a figure from before this date, expect a sharp drop; the earlier number was the inflated one. The old closure-based series still appears in the CSV export as “Closed rate %”.

Set the From and To dates at the top; changing either refetches immediately.

What the resolution time is made of

On the Agents tab, the panel What the resolution time is made of breaks “Avg to close” into the parts it is actually built from. It answers the common objection that the figure is unfair because it counts time nobody was working.

FigureWhat it counts
Total to closeThe full wall clock from the conversation being raised to it being closed. This has not changed — it is the same number as “Avg to close” in the table below.
− Waiting on customerEvery stretch where we had replied last and the next move was theirs.
− Waiting to be closedTime after the final message in the conversation — how long it sat finished before someone pressed Close.
= Active handlingWhat is left: the part that reflects how fast the conversation was actually worked.

The same two figures appear per agent in the table as — on customer and — unclosed, and in the CSV export.

The clock is never paused — that is deliberate

Waiting on the customer is not deducted from the headline. The real total stays the real total, and the waits are shown beside it so you can subtract them yourself when that is the fairer comparison. Quote whichever you mean, and say which one it is.

⚠ Closing discipline is usually the bigger half

Measured across 1,780 conversations closed in the week to 25 August 2026: of 42.7 hours to close, only 4.3 hours (10%) was waiting on the customer, while 28.1 hours (66%) was waiting to be closed. So a long “time to close” is usually not customers being slow — it is conversations finishing and staying open. Closing promptly is what moves this number.

If the panel is missing

It is calculated separately from the rest of the page, so on a very wide date range it can appear a moment after the tables. If it does not appear at all, the figures did not reconcile with the main report and it hides itself rather than show numbers that disagree — the rest of the report is unaffected.

⚠ Export is tickets only

The Export CSV button exports the ticket figures (product, platform, agent and a cost summary) from the Tickets and Calls tabs — there is no separate calls CSV. The Agents tab is the exception: there the button exports the agent conversation report, including both “Closed rate %” and “Resolution rate %”. In the agent call table, “missed to returned” counts missed calls that were later called back, not still-missed ones. This screen needs the reports permission.
