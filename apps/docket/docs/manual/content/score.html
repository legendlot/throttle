The on-time score says, for each team and each week, what share of the tasks people promised to finish that week were finished by the time they promised. It is deliberately fixed in advance: the list of promises is frozen when the week starts, so a score cannot be improved by quietly moving dates afterwards.

Measuring promises kept, by team, by week.

The cohort: what is counted

The week’s cohort is the list of tasks the score is about. It is taken at the very start of the week (just after midnight Indian time at the start of the week) and includes every open, top-level task whose deadline falls in that week. Recurring checklist items and sub-tasks are not part of it. The task’s deadline, team and owner as they were at that moment are frozen, so moving a task to another team later does not change whose score it is.

Joining mid-week

  • A task joins the week if it is given its first deadline inside the week: a new task created with a deadline this week, or an undated task dated for the first time.
  • A task created already done within the week counts as on time.
  • Re-dating a task into the week does not add it. If it slipped from an earlier week, that was the earlier week’s miss.
  • Re-dating a task out of the week does not remove it.

What counts as on time

OutcomeWhen
On timeAt the close of the week the task is done and was completed at or before the deadline it was committed with.
MissedAnything else in the cohort: still open, done late, re-dated and finished after the original time, abandoned during the week (a dropped promise is a miss), or done and then reopened and not finished again.

The score is on time ÷ committed, per team. For example, 18 of 20 committed tasks done in time is 90%.

Provisional and final

While the week is running, the review shows a PROVISIONAL score: how many were committed, how many are on time so far, how many have missed so far (their time has already passed) and how many are still due. The week closes at the end of its last day, and the score becomes FINAL and is stored unchanged. Opening and closing can lag by a few minutes. A week that was opened late says opened late, not exact; scores are exact from the week that began on 16 November 2026.

Who can see the score

There is no score on the task list or the dashboard. It is read in the Weekly Review (see the next chapters), and you see only what your access allows:

You areYou see
A team member or leadYour own team’s score, counting only tasks you can open.
AssociateAny team’s score, counting only tasks they can open.
FounderAny team, or all teams, with every task counted. Tasks in spaces they cannot open are included in the numbers and shown only as +N in spaces you’re not in.
ⓘ Why a deadline can’t be edited quietly

The first deadline is free to set; every later change goes through Revise with a reason, and is the only way the date moves. The review shows each slipped task with its original date, its new one, how many times it moved and the reasons given.
