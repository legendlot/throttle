Click a task anywhere in Docket and it opens in the task view: everything about that one task in a single place. By default it opens as a modal, a window over your list, and you can switch it to a side panel or a full page whenever you prefer. All three show the same task.

Modal, side panel, full page

Modal

The default. A centred window over a dimmed list. Click the dimmed area or press Esc to close it.

Side panel

A panel on the right while the list stays visible, scrollable and clickable behind it. Good for working down a list.

Full page

The task takes the whole screen inside Docket’s normal frame. The top-right button then reads Back to the list and returns you to the list with the task still open.

Switch with the Switch layout button in the task’s top bar (Open tasks as Modal, Side panel or Full page). Docket remembers your choice on this device. On a phone the task always fills the screen, with Details and Activity tabs at the top.

What’s on the task

The left side holds the task itself; the right side (or the Activity tab on a phone) holds the conversation.

SectionWhat it holds
Title and descriptionThe title (and, on a sub-task, a link up to its parent) and a free-text description. If you can edit the task, click either to change it.
Properties panelA tidy list of fields: Status, Owner, Collaborators, Deadline, Priority, Team, Program, Plan, Hand-off to, Space, Created (and Completed once done). Click a value to change it. A recurring checklist item shows Repeats in place of Deadline.
DependenciesWaiting on, Blocking and Related links to other tasks. Shown once something is linked (see below).
Sub-tasksThe child tasks, with their own owners and statuses, and a done/total count.
DocumentsLinks to outside files (Drive, Sheets, anything with a web address). Docket stores the link, not the file.
ActivityTwo tabs: Comments, with a box at the bottom to write a comment, and History, the full audit trail.

Sub-tasks and Documents are sections you can fold shut by clicking their headings.

Plan and hand-off properties

Two properties connect a task to the wider company, and both apply to top-level tasks only:

Plan

Click the value to search and pick a theme, milestone or result, or choose Unplanned to clear it. On a sub-task the field is read-only and shows the parent’s link, labelled from the parent task. See Linking Tasks to the Plan.

Hand-off to

Pick another team, and optionally a person in it. A status line reads Waiting for… to accept or Accepted by…, and the receiving team sees an Accept hand-off button here. You stay the owner. See Hand-offs.

Dependencies: waiting on, blocking, related

A dependency links two tasks so everyone can see what has to happen first. One link is shown from both ends: if DKT-12 is waiting on DKT-9, then DKT-9 shows as blocking DKT-12.

LinkWhat it means
Waiting onThis task cannot really finish until the other task is done. While at least one of those other tasks is still open, this task counts as waiting.
BlockingThe other end of the same link: other tasks are waiting for this one.
RelatedA plain cross-reference with no order and no rules. Useful for “see also”.

Links can join tasks in different spaces. Once you have linked at least one task, a Dependencies strip appears above the description, with a group for each kind (empty groups are hidden). Each row shows a status dot, the DKT- number, the title, the owner and the due date; click a row to open that task.

Checklists strip

When a task is tied to one or more record checklists (see Record Checklists), a Checklists strip appears under Dependencies. Each row reads like Launch: Badger · 6/11 (1 N/A) →; click it to open the record. A record in a space you can’t open shows only as 1 checklist you can’t see. With edit rights you can add one with + Checklist (beside the Description heading, or Link checklist on the strip), searching records by title or product, and remove one with the x. The record must be in the task’s space or in General. Recurring tasks have no Checklists strip.

Adding a dependency

  1. Open the task and choose Add dependency… in the More actions (…) menu. Three other doors lead to the same place: the + Add button on the Dependencies strip, the + Dependency button beside the Description heading when nothing is linked yet, and ⌘K then Add dependency to DKT-… while the task is open.
  2. Pick the kind: Waiting on…, Blocks… or Related to….
  3. Search for the other task by title or DKT- number (two or more characters, in every space you can open) and press Enter or click it. Docket confirms with Linked DKT-….

To remove a link, click the x at the end of its row (Remove link). You need edit rights on the task you are working in. Both adding and removing are written to the History tab of both tasks (Added dependency, Removed dependency). When the last open blocker of a task is finished or abandoned, the waiting task gets an Unblocked line in its History.

Finished and abandoned blockers

A blocker counts as cleared once it is Done. An abandoned blocker also counts as cleared, but its row is tagged Blocker abandoned so the waiting owner knows the work was dropped, not completed. Cleared links stay on the strip as a record.

Marking a waiting task done

Docket warns you but does not stop you. If you mark a task done while it is still waiting on open tasks, a confirmation appears, for example DKT-9 is still waiting on: DKT-4 Print run. Mark done anyway? Confirm to finish it, or cancel to leave it as it is. The same warning appears when you tick it in the list, drag it on the board or use it in a bulk update (3 of these are waiting on open tasks. Mark all done anyway?).

Date clash hint

If a task is waiting on a blocker that is due after the task itself, the blocker’s row shows an amber line such as DKT-123 is due 30 Nov, after this task (25 Nov). It is only a hint. Docket never moves a date for you, because every deadline change needs a reason.

Tasks you can’t see

If a linked task lives in a space you cannot open, it appears only as 1 task you can’t see (or 3 tasks you can’t see). No title, number or owner is shown, but it still counts towards waiting.

ⓘ Waiting and Blocked are two different things

The Blocked status is still a manual choice you make in the Status picker; Docket never sets it for you, and a waiting task keeps whatever status it has. Nothing about a dependency changes a deadline or a status automatically. Reviewers can see links but not change them.

Editing

Every property is click-to-edit in the panel: click it, pick the new value, and it saves. The title and description save when you click away. You can only edit a task you own, created, or (as an admin) any task. Reviewers see all of this without the edit controls.

With focus outside a text box, these keys work inside an open task: Shift+S status, Shift+A owner, Shift+D deadline, Shift+P priority, s add a sub-task.

The deadline, and why it is locked

Under Deadline the panel shows the current date, and, once it has been moved, a REV tag with the number of revisions plus a line reading Original … · locked. The first date you set is the original promise and never changes. To move the deadline, open it and enter the new date and a required reason; the button reads Set the first time and Revise after that.

⛔ Abandoning is the only way to stop a task

There is no delete button anywhere in Docket. If a task should not be done, choose Abandon… (in the Status picker or the More actions menu) and give a reason; it is required and recorded. The task stays visible with a banner reading Abandoned and your reason. An abandoned task is read-only until it is reactivated: it cannot be edited, re-dated, re-parented, moved, commented on, or have collaborators or documents changed, and the server refuses any such change. The comment box is replaced by This task is abandoned — reactivate it to comment. You also cannot add a sub-task under it. Reactivating (a status change) is the one thing that works, and an admin can still delete a comment on it. The owner, creator or an admin can use the banner’s Reactivate button, which is also logged.

Comments and History

The Comments tab is a running discussion on the task. Type in the box at the bottom and press Comment; you can edit or delete your own comments. The History tab is the unchangeable record: every status change, every deadline revision (with its reason), every owner or priority change, every collaborator or document added or removed. You cannot edit history; that is the point of it.

✓ Share a task with a link

Use Copy link to send someone a direct link to the task; it opens on the full page. An address of the form /tasks?task=… opens the task over the Tasks list instead, which is also how Docket returns you to the list after you leave the full page. Either way the person only sees the task if they are allowed to see it.

ⓘ Moving a task to another space

The Space property lets the owner move a task into a different space, including a private one you belong to. Moving a task changes who can see it. Sub-tasks always follow their parent’s space.
