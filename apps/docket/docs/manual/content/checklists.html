Checklists are your recurring work: the things you do every day, every Monday, or on the first of the month, that don’t deserve a fresh task each time. You set them up once with a repeat schedule, and they show up on your own checklist whenever they’re due, ready to tick off.

What it’s for
Routine, repeating work you tick off.
Whose it is
One person’s checklist at a time.
Who
Member Reviewer Admin
Find it

Checklists vs Tasks

A normal task happens once and has a deadline. A checklist item happens again and again on a schedule and has no deadline, just a repeat rule. Because of that, checklist items are kept completely separate from the Tasks board:

ⓘ Recurring work stays off the board

Checklist items never appear on the Tasks list or the Dashboard, and they don’t count toward anyone’s task totals. They live only on the /checklist page. If you open one in the task view it shows Repeats (its schedule) where a task would show a deadline.

The layout

The Checklist screen has three tabs along the top: My day, Manage and Oversight. Under each, work is grouped under collapsible headers, the same style as the Tasks list.

My day

What is due on the day you are viewing. The Today group lists every item due, ready to tick, with a done count (for example 3/5). Below it come any checklist templates assigned to you, and a Recurring this week group previewing the rest of this week’s items with the days they fall on (read-only: you tick an item on its own day). Arrows beside the date step back to earlier days; the Today button brings you back. A Today | Register switch at the top flips the same checklist into a chart (see The Register below).

Manage

Where you set things up. My recurring tasks lists every recurring item you own with its schedule, and a New button to add one. Checklist templates below it holds reusable multi-section checklists for a role, with a New template button.

Oversight

For managers: per-person checklist adherence for a chosen date, covering people you manage or have assigned a checklist to. Click a person to see their run for that day, read-only.

Managers and reviewers who can view other people’s checklists also get a person picker on My day, to see how a report is tracking (read-only, see below).

Creating a checklist item

  1. Open Manage and click New. Give the item a title (e.g. “Check the cold-storage temperature log”), and optionally set who it is assigned to, its priority, team and a description.
  2. Choose how often it repeats with the schedule picker: Daily, Weekly (tick the weekdays, e.g. Mon, Wed, Fri) or Monthly (a day of the month; in shorter months it falls on the last day).
  3. Set a time of day. The time it should be done by, used only to order your day, it doesn’t nag you.
  4. Click Add. From now on it appears under Today whenever the schedule says it’s due.
✓ Optional end date

The schedule picker has an optional Ends date. Set one for a routine that should only run for a while (a temporary daily check during a launch, say), and the item auto-drops off your checklist once that date passes, no need to remember to retire it.

To change a schedule later, click the pencil beside the item in Manage. It opens the same schedule picker as a small popover; choose the new pattern and click Save.

Ticking things off

On the My day tab, tick the item to mark it done for that day; the progress count goes up and the tick sticks across reloads (it records a completion for that date). Made a mistake? Un-tick it and the completion is removed. Each day is its own slate, ticking today has no effect on yesterday or tomorrow.

The Register

At the top of My day is a Today | Register switch; Docket remembers your choice on this device. Today is the list described above. Register is a paper-style chart: your items run down the left, the dates they were due run across the top, and each item gets one cell per day. It shows the same ticks as Today, so ticking in either view is the same tick.

Window

7D, 14D or 30D (it opens on 14D). Use the ‹ › arrows to page back and forward, and Today to jump back. You can never page past today. Only dates on which at least one item was due become columns, so a Monday-only weekly item shows just Mondays.

Cells

Green tick: done. Amber tick: done late (after the section’s due time, or the task’s set time). Hollow tick: ticked later, filled in on a later day; it still counts, but the chart shows it was back-filled. Red cross: missed (due, the day is over, not ticked). Empty box: due today, not ticked yet. Dot: not scheduled that day. Hover a cell to see when it was ticked and by whom (on shared charts, also who it was done for).

Adherence

A percentage at the end of every row (that item), a number at the foot of every column (that day), and the chart’s overall percentage with done/due in its header. Today only counts once ticked, because the day isn’t over. Green is 90 and above, amber 60 to 89, red below 60.

Blocks

One block per chart: Recurring tasks (your own recurring tasks) first, then every checklist template assigned to you.

A tick is never lost when an item’s schedule changes later: a past tick on a day the new schedule skips still shows, and still counts, on that day.

Ticking from the Register: click a cell on your own checklist, for today or any past day the item was due. Un-tick the same way. Future days can never be ticked, on either view (the server refuses it too), and neither can a day the item isn’t scheduled.

ⓘ Viewing someone else’s Register

Managers and Reviewers who can view a person’s checklist through the person selector see that person’s Register too, read-only.

Shared charts

A checklist template normally gives each assigned person their own chart. In Manage, the template editor has a Chart setting: Each person their own (the default) or One shared chart. A shared chart is one chart for a place or a line (a washroom, say): whoever is assigned ticks it, and the tick records who. Shared charts carry a Shared chart tag on My day and in the Register.

On a shared chart an assignee can type a name into “Ticking for someone? Name them” before ticking. This is for a supervisor ticking on behalf of floor staff who have no Docket login; the cell then shows “for <name>” on hover. Only the person who ticked it, or the checklist’s creator or an admin, can clear a shared tick: a tick is the record. A shared cell someone else ticked shows as read-only for the other assignees. If two people tick the same shared item at the same moment, the first tick stands.

ⓘ The Chart setting locks once used

A checklist’s Chart setting (own or shared) can’t be switched once anything on it has been ticked.

Assigning a checklist item to someone else

You can create a checklist item for another person, it lands on their checklist, not yours, and they tick it off. Use this to hand someone a recurring responsibility (a daily open/close routine, a weekly stock check).

⚠ Only the owner ticks their own items

A manager or reviewer can open a report’s checklist through the person picker to see how they’re doing, but cannot tick items on their behalf, the person who owns the item is the only one who marks it done. (Shared charts are the exception: any assignee ticks them.) This keeps a checklist an honest record of what that person actually completed.

Editing & retiring

Click an item’s title to open it and change its title or details, or use the pencil in Manage to change the schedule. When a routine is no longer needed, use Stop (the circle-slash button) in Manage. It asks for a reason, works like abandoning a task, and the item stops appearing under Today; the history of what was completed is kept.
