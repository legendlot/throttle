The Tasks list is where you live. It is one screen that captures new work, shows what is on your plate, and lets you edit almost everything inline without opening anything. This chapter is the longest in the manual because it covers the screen you will use most.

What it’s for
Capturing, finding, and updating tasks.
Read or act?
Both: read your list and edit in place.
Who
Member Reviewer Admin
Shows
The tasks you’re allowed to see, in one space.

Capturing a task in one line

At the very top is a single box: “Add a task. Type a title and press Enter…”. Type a title, press Enter, and the task exists. That is the whole capture step, no form, no required fields. The new task lands in The Grid so you can finish it off later.

✓ Capture first, tidy later

Do not stop to fill in the owner and deadline while an idea is fresh. Get the title down, hit Enter, and move on. The Grid will remind you what still needs finishing.

The Grid: tasks that need setting up

Just-captured tasks sit in a tinted tray at the top of the list labelled THE GRID · NEEDS SETUP. A task is in the Grid whenever it is missing an owner or a deadline (or both). The moment it has both, it leaves the Grid on its own and joins the main Active board below. Think of the Grid as your inbox of half-formed tasks.

Each task is one row. The columns, left to right:

Editing without opening anything

This is the heart of the screen. If you are allowed to edit a task, most cells are click-to-edit right in the row:

  1. Click a property cell (Team, Program, Owner, Status, or Priority) and it turns into a dropdown. Pick a value and it saves instantly.
  2. Press Tab to walk across the row. Once you are editing one cell, Tab moves to the next and commits your choice as it goes, so you can set several fields with the keyboard in a few keystrokes.
  3. Click the Deadline cell to open a calendar. The first time, you just pick a date. After that, changing it asks for a reason (see below).
  4. Click the title to rename it; click the DKT- number to open the full panel instead.
  5. Click the People cell to add or remove collaborators from a small popup.
⚠ Revising a deadline always needs a reason

The first deadline you put on a task is its locked original promise, set with no questions asked. Every change after that asks for a short reason and is recorded on the task's history. This is on purpose: it is how Docket can always answer “why did this slip?”. See The Task Lifecycle for the full story.

ⓘ If a row won’t let you edit

You can only change the core fields of tasks you own or created (admins can change any). On tasks you merely collaborate on or can only view, the cells simply will not turn into editors. That is expected, not a glitch.

Sub-tasks

A task can hold a list of sub-tasks (one level deep, sub-tasks cannot themselves have sub-tasks). When a task has them, a small chevron and a done/total count appear next to its title. Click the chevron to expand the children as indented ↳ rows underneath. To add one, hover the parent row and click the +; type a title and press Enter. New sub-tasks inherit the parent's team and owner, which you can change afterwards. The Expand all / Collapse all buttons in the control bar open or close every task's sub-tasks at once.

Finding tasks: search, filter, group, sort

Four tools sit above the list:

Search

The box on the left (press / to jump in) filters as you type, instantly. It matches the ID, title, owner, team, creator and collaborator names, and every word you type must match somewhere.

Manage

The Manage button opens a panel of filters: by status, team, owner, priority and program, plus quick toggles for My tasks, Overdue and Revised. A small number on the button tells you how many filters are active; Clear all resets them.

Group by

Inside the Manage panel, Group by reflows the board into sections by Owner, Team or Program. Each section can be collapsed.

Sort

Click any column header to sort by it. Click again to reverse, and a third time to switch sorting off. Sorting applies to both top-level rows and their sub-tasks.

List view and Board view (Kanban)

The list can be shown two ways. The small two-button switch on the right of the control bar flips between them, and Docket remembers your choice.

List view

The default: tasks as rows, with The Grid, inline editing, sub-tasks and bulk select, all as described above.

Board view

A Kanban: tasks become cards arranged in columns. By default the columns are the four statuses (Not started, In progress, Blocked, Done). Use Group by in the Manage panel to switch the columns to Owner, Team or Program instead.

To move a task, drag its card into another column. On a status board, dragging a card to Done marks it done; on an Owner board it reassigns the owner, and so on. Only cards you are allowed to edit can be dragged. Click a card to open the task panel as usual.

ⓘ What the board shows

Each card shows the title, DKT- number, owner, priority, deadline and any team or program tags, plus small sub-task and comment counts. Only top-level tasks appear as cards (a parent shows its sub-task count rather than separate cards), and Abandoned tasks are left off the board. The Grid, Archive done, Select and the compact/roomy control are list-view tools and are hidden on the board. Board view also works inside a Program view.

Updating many tasks at once

When you need to make the same change to several tasks (assign them all to one person, push them all to next week, bump them all to P1), use bulk update instead of editing each row by hand. It works in both The Grid and the main board.

  1. Click Select in the control bar. A tick box appears on the left of every task you are allowed to edit. Tasks you only collaborate on or can view show a faint, locked box: you cannot pick those.
  2. Tick the tasks you want. Pick them one by one, or use Select all to grab every editable task currently shown (your search and filters decide what “all” means).
  3. Use the yellow bar that pops up at the bottom. It shows how many are selected and gives one button each for Owner, Status, Priority, Deadline and Program.
  4. Pick a value and it applies to every selected task at once. Clear deselects everything; clicking Select again leaves bulk mode.
ⓘ Only your tasks change

A bulk action only touches tasks you are allowed to edit, which is why collaborator-only tasks cannot be ticked in the first place. If a few in your selection still cannot be changed, Docket updates the rest and tells you how many it skipped.

⚠ Bulk deadlines still keep the audit trail

Setting a deadline in bulk follows the same rule as a single task: tasks getting their first deadline are set silently, but if any selected task already has one, Docket asks for a single reason and records the change on each task's history.

Which space am I in?

The list always shows one space at a time. By default that is General, the open company-wide space. If you click a private space in the sidebar, the list shows only that space's tasks and a header with the space name appears; anything you capture then lands in that space. Spaces have their own chapter.

My tasks: everything on your plate, across every space

My tasks in the sidebar is different from the rest of the list. Instead of one space, it pulls together every task you are involved in across all the spaces you can see, and splits them into two sections:

Assigned to me

Tasks where you are the owner, the person accountable for getting them done. This is the top section.

Collaborating

Tasks where you are a collaborator but not the owner. This sits in a collapsible section below; fold it away when you want to focus only on what you own.

Because it spans spaces, each row carries a Space column so you can see where a task lives. Capturing a task here assigns it to you automatically, and search, filters and bulk select all work the same as on a normal list.

⚒ A typical pass through your list

Open Tasks. Glance at The Grid, set an owner and deadline on anything sitting there. Toggle My tasks in Manage to see only your own. Sort by Deadline to bring the soonest to the top, and clear anything flagged overdue in red. Click a title to open it when you need comments or sub-tasks.
