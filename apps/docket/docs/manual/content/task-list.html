The Tasks list is where you live. It is one screen that captures new work, shows what is on your plate, and lets you edit almost everything in the row without opening anything. Tasks are laid out as a tidy list of rows, grouped under collapsible headers, with columns you can show, hide and resize. This chapter is the longest in the manual because it covers the screen you will use most.

What it’s for
Capturing, finding, and updating tasks.
Read or act?
Both: read your list and edit in place.
Who
Member Reviewer Admin
Shows
The tasks you’re allowed to see, in one space (or across spaces on My tasks).

The layout

From top to bottom the screen has:

Views bar

A List / Board switch, a greyed-out + View (saved views are coming later), and on the right a Copy link button. In a private space it also has a space dashboard button, and, for the space’s owner, a space settings button.

Toolbar

On the left, Group and Subtasks. On the right, Filter, Me, Archive done, Select, the search box, Columns, Customize and the yellow + Task button. On a phone the labelled buttons shrink to icons.

The list

The column header row, then the groups of tasks, each with its own header and a + Add task line at the foot.

Capturing a task in one line

Click + Task (or press c, or use + New in the top bar) and a New task line opens under the toolbar. Type a title, press Enter, and the task exists. That is the whole capture step, no form, no required fields. The new task lands in The Grid so you can finish it off later.

Quick-add: type it, don’t click it

The capture line (the top + Task line, each group’s + Add task line, and a Board column’s add line) reads a few shortcuts out of the title and turns them into Owner, Deadline and Priority chips before you save, so you can check what Docket understood.

TypeYou get
@riyaOwner. A first name, or a prefix that matches only one person. If two people fit, it stays as plain text.
!p0 to !p3Priority P0 to P3.
today, tomorrow / tmrwThat day.
by fri, fri 5pm, or a weekday as the last wordThe next such weekday. A weekday in the middle of a sentence (“Sat with Riya”) stays text.
12 nov, nov 12, 12/11A date; slash dates are day/month. 12/11 counts only if it is a future date within 60 days, so “3/4 sleeve” stays text.
5pm, 5:30pm, 17:00A time (Indian time). A date with no time means 6 pm.

The words that became a chip leave the title. Docket never sets a deadline in the past: a time that has already passed today rolls to tomorrow, and a date that cannot be read as a future one stays in the title. Pick a value from a chip yourself and it wins over the typed words, which go back into the title. Click the × on a chip to drop it and keep the words as text.

You can also capture straight into a group: every group ends with a + Add task line, and the task is created already carrying that group’s value (grouped by Owner, a new task gets that owner; grouped by Team, that team; grouped by Status, a task added under In progress is created in that status straight away). The group header has an Add task button too, which jumps you to that line. With a row focused, c opens its own group’s line.

✓ Capture first, tidy later

Do not stop to fill in the owner and deadline while an idea is fresh. Get the title down, hit Enter, and move on. The Grid will remind you what still needs finishing.

The Grid: tasks that need setting up

Just-captured tasks sit in a dashed yellow group at the top of the list called The Grid, with the hint needs an owner and a deadline to hit the board. A task is in the Grid whenever it is missing an owner or a deadline (or both). Each Grid card has two small chips, Owner and Deadline: click one to fill it in right there. The moment a task has both, it leaves the Grid on its own and joins the main list below. Click the Grid’s header to fold it away.

Triage The Grid

When the Grid has tasks you can edit, its header shows a Triage N button. It walks you through them one at a time, so a backlog of half-finished tasks takes minutes instead of a lot of clicking. Each card shows the task and the fields still needed; set the Owner and Deadline (you can type something like fri 5pm into the deadline box) and a progress line at the top tracks how far you are.

KeyWhat it does
EnterSave & next.
AAbandon the task. A reason is required and logged.

EscClose. The list refreshes when you close.

Only the fields you actually touched are saved, and a deadline in the past is refused. A task that has both an owner and a deadline after saving leaves the Grid; one that still lacks one stays. Tasks you cannot edit are skipped.

Reading a row

Each task is one row, 36 pixels tall by default. At the left is a round tick circle (click it to mark the task done, or not done), then the title. The columns to the right are:

ColumnWhat it shows
NameThe title, with the permanent DKT- number beside it (turn the numbers off in Customize). A REV tag means the deadline has been changed at least once. A sub-task count like 2/5 shows done out of total.
StatusNot started / In progress / Done / Blocked / Abandoned. Always shown.
TeamThe department it belongs to.
PlanThe plan item the task is linked to, shown as its code (hover for the title). An empty cell offers Set plan. See Linking Tasks to the Plan.
SpaceWhich space it lives in. Appears only on My tasks, which spans several spaces.
OwnerThe accountable person, as an initial circle (hover for the full name).
CollaboratorsHelpers, as small overlapping initial circles. Hover to see names.
PriorityA flag for P0 (immediate) to P3 (low).
DeadlineWhen it is due. Turns red once the date has passed on an open task.
ActivitySmall counts of attached documents and comments.

The dependency icon

A task that is part of a dependency shows a small icon beside its title, on list rows and on board cards:

  • Red “waiting” icon with a number: the task is waiting on that many open tasks.
  • “Blocking” icon with a number: that many open tasks are waiting for this one.
  • Grey tick: the task had blockers and every one is now cleared (done or abandoned).

Hover for a one-line summary. Click to open a small popover listing the linked tasks: click one to open it, or use the x to remove a link if you can edit the task. Open DKT-… to add links at the bottom takes you into the task, where new links are added (see Inside a Task). Tasks with no links show no icon. Marking a waiting task done asks for confirmation first, from the tick circle, the board and bulk updates alike.

Shaping the columns

  • Show or hide: the Columns button lists every optional column with a tick. Name and Status are always on. Reset columns puts everything back to the defaults.
  • Resize: drag the thin edge on the right of a column header.
  • Reorder: drag a column header onto another one.
  • Sort: click a header (Name, Status, Owner, Priority, Deadline). Click again to reverse, and a third time to switch sorting off.

Docket remembers your column choices on this device.

Customize: row density and ID numbers

The Customize button (the sliders icon) sets how roomy the list is: Compact (32 pixel rows), Default (36) or Roomy (44). Under Show it also toggles the DKT- ids and the Team column. On the Board, Customize sets card size instead (below).

Editing without opening anything

This is the heart of the screen. If you are allowed to edit a task, most cells are click-to-edit right in the row:

  1. Click a property cell (Status, Team, Plan, Owner or Priority) and a small picker opens, with a search box for the long lists. Pick a value and it saves instantly. An empty cell shows a faint icon when you hover it.
  2. Click the Deadline cell to open a calendar. The first time, you just pick a date. After that, changing it asks for a reason (see below).
  3. Click the Collaborators cell’s small + to add a helper from a short list.
  4. Click the row itself (the title or empty space) to open the task. See Inside a Task.
  5. Use the row’s buttons. Hover a row to reveal + (add a sub-task) and … (Open, Copy link, Add sub-task, Mark done, Abandon).
⚠ Revising a deadline always needs a reason

The first deadline you put on a task is its locked original promise, set with no questions asked. Every change after that asks for a short reason and is recorded on the task’s history. This is on purpose: it is how Docket can always answer “why did this slip?”. See The Task Lifecycle for the full story.

ⓘ If a row won’t let you edit

You can only change the core fields of tasks you own or created (admins can change any). On tasks you merely collaborate on or can only view, the cells simply will not turn into editors. Abandoned tasks are read-only too. That is expected, not a glitch.

Keyboard on the list

Click a row to focus it (or Tab to it), then:

  • ↓ ↑ (or j k) move between rows, and Enter opens the task.
  • x (or Space) toggles done.
  • → / ← (or l / h) expand or collapse its sub-tasks, and s adds a sub-task.
  • f opens Filter and g opens the Group menu.

Sub-tasks

A task can hold a list of sub-tasks (one level deep, sub-tasks cannot themselves have sub-tasks). When a task has them, an arrow and a done/total count appear next to its title; click the arrow to open the children as indented rows underneath. To add one, hover the parent row and click + (or press s); type a title and press Enter. New sub-tasks inherit the parent’s team and owner, which you can change afterwards.

The Subtasks button in the toolbar has Expand all and Collapse all, and a choice between showing sub-tasks Nested under parent (the default) or As separate tasks, where every sub-task gets a row of its own. It appears once at least one task has sub-tasks.

Groups

The Group button on the left of the toolbar regroups the list by Owner, Team, Status or Week ending (or No grouping). Week ending gives one group per week, with Overdue first and No date last; see Weeks and Week Ending. Each group has a header with its name and a count. Click the header to collapse or expand the group; its … menu also offers Collapse all groups and Expand all groups. Docket remembers which groups you folded. An x on the Group button clears the grouping.

Finding tasks: search and filter

Search

The box on the right of the toolbar (press / to jump in) filters as you type, instantly. It matches the ID, title, owner, team, creator and collaborator names, and every word you type must match somewhere. For a search across spaces and screens use the command bar (⌘K).

Filter

Opens a small panel to filter by Status, Team, Owner, Priority, Plan (a theme, milestone or result, or Unplanned) and Week (This week, Next week, Week ending…, Overdue, No date), with quick Overdue and Revised toggles, and a Dependencies pair: Waiting on others (tasks still waiting on an open blocker) and Blocking others (tasks others are still waiting on). Only one of the two can be on at a time, and a blocker that is already done or abandoned does not count. A number on the button says how many filters are on; Clear filters resets them.

Me

A one-click toggle that limits the list to tasks you own, are assigned, collaborate on or raised.

Archive done

Moves finished tasks out of the main list into a collapsed Archived section at the bottom, so the list shows only live work. Untick a task there to bring it back.

List view and Board view

The List / Board switch in the views bar flips between the two, and Docket remembers your choice. (My tasks is list-only.)

List view

The default: tasks as rows, with The Grid, inline editing, sub-tasks and bulk select, all as described above.

Board view

A Kanban: tasks become cards in columns. By default the columns are the statuses (Not started, In progress, Blocked, Done). Use Group to switch the columns to Owner, Team or Week ending instead. The week columns are read-only: a card cannot be dragged to another week, because a deadline only moves by revising it with a reason.

To move a task, drag its card into another column. On a status board, dragging a card to Done marks it done; on an Owner board it reassigns the owner, and so on. Only cards you are allowed to edit move. From the keyboard, focus a card and press Shift + ← / → to move it one column; plain arrows walk between cards, and Enter opens the task.

  • Collapse a column: use the column’s … menu, then Collapse column. It folds to a thin strip with the card count; click the strip to bring it back.
  • Add a card: each column has its own add line, and the new task is created with that column’s value.
  • Card size: Customize offers Small, Medium and Large cards, and a toggle for the DKT- ids.
ⓘ What the board shows

Each card shows the title, DKT- number, owner, priority, deadline and any team, plan or space tags and a hand-off chip, plus small sub-task, comment and document counts. Only top-level tasks appear as cards (a parent shows its sub-task count rather than separate cards), and Abandoned tasks are left off the board. Columns, Archive done, Select and row density are list-only tools.

Plan, week and hand-off on the list

Three ideas from The Plan part show up on every list:

  • Plan. The Plan column and the Plan chip on board cards show the goal a task is linked to; the Plan filter narrows to a theme, milestone, result or Unplanned. On the capture line, a Plan chip sits beside Owner, Deadline and Priority once there is something to link to. Only top-level tasks carry a link; sub-tasks inherit their parent’s.
  • Week. Group › Week ending and the Week filter work on the List, the Grid and the Board.
  • Hand-off. A task given to another team shows a small chip such as → Design on its row, grid card and board card, amber until it is accepted. The same task appears in the receiving team’s Incoming section on My tasks.

Updating many tasks at once

When you need to make the same change to several tasks (assign them all to one person, push them all to next week, bump them all to P1), use bulk update instead of editing each row by hand. It works in The Grid and in the main list.

  1. Click Select in the toolbar (or tick any row’s box). A tick box appears on the left of every task you are allowed to edit. Tasks you only collaborate on or can view show a faint, locked box: you cannot pick those.
  2. Tick the tasks you want. Pick them one by one, or use Select all to grab every editable task currently shown (your search and filters decide what “all” means).
  3. Use the bar that pops up at the bottom. It shows how many are selected and has one button each for Owner, Status, Priority, Deadline, Collaborators (add or remove), Move (to another space) and Abandon.
  4. Pick a value and it applies to every selected task at once. The x at the left of the bar clears the selection; clicking Select again leaves bulk mode.
ⓘ Only your tasks change

A bulk action only touches tasks you are allowed to edit, which is why collaborator-only tasks cannot be ticked in the first place. If a few in your selection still cannot be changed, Docket updates the rest and tells you how many it skipped.

⚠ Bulk deadlines still keep the audit trail

Setting a deadline in bulk follows the same rule as a single task: tasks getting their first deadline are set silently, but if any selected task already has one, Docket asks for a single reason and records the change on each task’s history. Abandoning in bulk likewise needs one reason.

Which space am I in?

The list shows one space at a time. By default that is General, the open company-wide space. If you click a private space in the sidebar, the breadcrumb shows its name (with a padlock) and the list shows only that space’s tasks; anything you capture lands in that space. Spaces have their own chapter. (Programs work differently: opening a program shows its tasks from every space you can access.)

My tasks: everything on your plate, across every space

My tasks on the rail is different from the rest of the list. Instead of one space, it pulls together every task you are involved in across all the spaces you can see, in four groups (empty groups are hidden):

Today & overdue

Open tasks you own whose deadline is today or earlier, with the most overdue first. “Today” runs to the end of the day in Indian time, and the group re-sorts itself if you leave the tab open past midnight.

Assigned to me

The rest of the tasks you own. Its + Add task line creates a task already assigned to you.

Collaborating

Tasks where you are a collaborator but not the owner.

Raised by me

Open tasks you created that someone else owns, so you can keep an eye on what you handed off.

When another team has handed a task to you or your team, an Incoming section appears above these groups, with To you and To your team tabs and an Accept button on each. It is hidden when nothing is incoming. See Hand-offs.

Because it spans spaces, each row carries a Space column so you can see where a task lives. Every task is its own row here (no nesting of sub-tasks). Search, filters and bulk select work as on a normal list.

Clean up: the stale-work sweep

The Clean up N button at the top of My tasks opens /tasks?lens=stale, a list of your open work (sub-tasks included, recurring tasks not) that is overdue by 30 days or more or untouched for 60 days or more. Each row says why: 145d over or untouched 154d. A task that is both appears once, under Overdue.

Done

Marks the task done.

Revise

Pick a new deadline and give a reason (logged). A past date is refused. If the task never had a deadline the button reads Set date and no reason is asked.

Abandon

Stops the task; a reason is required.

Tick several rows for a bulk Done all or Abandon all (one reason for all). On a phone the row actions sit in a … menu. People with view-all (Reviewers and admins) get a Mine | Everyone switch; Everyone groups the list by owner with an owner filter, but covers only spaces you can access (private spaces need membership). The sweep is pull-only: nothing is sent to anyone.

⚒ A typical pass through your list

Open My tasks and clear Today & overdue first. Then glance at The Grid on the Tasks list and set an owner and deadline on anything sitting there. Sort by Deadline to bring the soonest to the top, and clear anything shown in red. Click a task to open it when you need comments or sub-tasks. Once in a while, use Clean up to retire or re-date work that has gone stale.
