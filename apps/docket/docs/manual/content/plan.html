The Plan is where the company writes down what it is trying to achieve, in four levels, from a broad theme down to the everyday task. Linking your tasks to it answers a question the task list alone cannot: “does this work move one of our goals?”. The Plan sits above the task list; it never replaces it.

The four levels

LevelWhat it isExample
ThemeA broad direction the company is working towards. Rarely changes.Grow online sales
MilestoneA dated outcome with one owner, inside a theme.M1 Launch the new website, owner Priya, due 30 Nov
ResultOne checkable piece of a milestone, with its own owner and date.M1.R1 Product pages are live

Each theme, milestone and result has a short code (such as M1 or M1.R1) so people can quote it. In the task pickers an item reads as a chain: Grow online sales › M1 › M1.R1.

The two ways to open it

Click Plan in the icon rail, or the first row of the sidebar. What you see depends on who you are:

Plan register

For Founders: every theme, with its milestones and results underneath, including targets and founders-only themes. Click any row to open it in a side panel and edit it. Founders add a theme with + Theme, a milestone with the Milestone button on a theme, and a result with Result on a milestone. The up and down arrows on a row change its order. A Founders’ Associate sees the same register read-only (no targets, no founders-only themes).

My milestones

For everyone else: the milestones you own, grouped under their theme, with the results beneath each. If a result of yours sits under someone else’s milestone, it appears in Results I own on other milestones. If you own nothing yet, you see You don’t own any milestones yet with a Propose button.

Each row shows the code, the title, the owner, the due date, a Re-dated tag if the date has moved, and a coloured state chip. A padlock marks a founders-only theme. The buttons at the top are Three weeks (see Plan Views), Propose (see Proposals, Measures & Targets) and, for Founders, + Theme.

States

Milestones and results move through a few states, shown as a chip on the row:

StateMeaning
ProposedA draft. Someone has suggested it, or a Founder has made it without agreeing it yet. The date is only a draft.
AgreedOwner, date and the “done when” sentence are settled. The due date is now locked (see below).
DoneFinished, with a line of evidence saying how we know.
DroppedDeliberately stopped, with a reason. Nothing is deleted.
SplitReplaced by two or more smaller ones (below). The original is greyed out and read-only, and points to its parts, for example Split → M4, M5.

A theme is simply Active or Retired.

ⓘ Nothing live under something closed

A milestone can only be agreed under a theme that is active, and a result only under an agreed milestone. You cannot drop or finish a milestone while results or tasks under it are still live; Docket tells you to drop or finish those first.

What you can do to an item

Open an item and the Actions row shows only what you are allowed to do in its current state.

ActionWhat it asks for
AgreeAn owner, a due date and a “done when” sentence. The due date locks.
Re-dateA new due date and a reason. See the note below.
Mark doneEvidence: what shows it is done, such as a link, a number or a note.
DropA reason.
ReopenA reason. Brings a done or dropped item back.
SplitFounders only. See below.

The panel also lists the item’s history (who did what, when, in Indian time) and the tasks linked to it that you can open.

⚠ Re-dating an agreed milestone needs a reason

Once a milestone is agreed, its due date cannot simply be edited. Use Re-date, pick the new date and write why. The original date is kept (the panel shows was 30 Nov), the row gets a Re-dated tag, and the move is recorded in the history and in the weekly review. This is the plan’s version of the audited task deadline.

Who can do what

  • Founders can do everything above, on every level.
  • The owner of a milestone can add results under it, and can edit, re-date and mark done the results under it. Changes to the milestone itself (agreeing, re-dating, finishing, dropping) are the Founders’.
  • The owner of a result can edit it and mark it done. Re-dating it, or changing its owner, is for the milestone’s owner or a Founder.
  • Everyone else reads, links their tasks, and proposes.

Splitting a milestone or result

When one agreed milestone turns out to be really two or three, a Founder uses Split instead of editing it. A split makes 2 to 10 new agreed parts and retires the original, all in one step:

  1. Give a reason, then fill in each part: title, owner and due date. Add or remove parts with the buttons beneath them.
  2. Decide where each result goes. Every live result under it is given to one of the parts, or dropped (with a reason for the dropped ones).
  3. Decide where the open tasks go. Pick a default part for linked open tasks, and override it for any task that belongs elsewhere. Tasks in spaces you cannot open go to the default part.
  4. Press Split. Everything moves together, and every item and task gets a history line. Done and abandoned tasks keep their old link.

Who sees what in the Plan

The Plan is deliberately not an open book. Everyone sees the shape of the company’s goals, but the targets and the detail stay with the people who need them.

You areYou see
Founder
(super admin)		Everything: every theme, milestone and result, the Target on each, and the founders-only themes.
Associate
(Founders’ Associate)		Every item that is not founders-only, in full, except targets. Read-only.
An ownerThe milestones you own and the results under them, plus the title of every theme that is not founders-only. No targets.
Everyone elseThe title of every theme that is not founders-only. Nothing below it unless you own it.

A founders-only (restricted) theme is marked with a padlock and is invisible to everyone except Founders, together with everything under it. Founders switch it on with the Restricted tick box on the theme.

ⓘ Linking to someone else’s milestone

You can link your task to a milestone you do not own only if you can see it. If a task you can open is linked to a milestone you cannot see, its chip simply reads Another owner’s milestone, and hovering shows only the theme. A task linked to a founders-only item shows no plan chip at all to anyone outside that circle.

ⓘ The Founders’ space

Tasks linked to a founders-only item live in a special Founders’ space that only the Founders can open. Its tasks are never named in a Slack message, a copied review summary or anyone else’s list. Other people only ever see them as a count, such as “+3 in spaces you’re not in”.
