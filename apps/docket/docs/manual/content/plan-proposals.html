If something belongs on the plan and is not there, anyone can say so. A proposal goes to the Founders, who accept or decline it, and you always find out which, and why. This chapter also covers how a milestone says it is measured, and the one place Docket nudges a Founder to close something off.

What it’s for
Raising a milestone or a risk; measuring one.
Who proposes
Anyone with a Docket login.
Who decides
Founder
Where
Propose button on /plan

Proposing something

  1. Press Propose at the top of the Plan page. A panel opens on the right.
  2. Fill it in. What (a short title) and Why it matters are required; the Founders read the why when they decide. Under It is a… choose Milestone: something to deliver or Risk: something that could go wrong. Theme and By when are optional hints.
  3. Press Send proposal. You can have up to 20 waiting at a time.

Your proposals appear under My proposals on the Plan page, each marked Waiting, Accepted or Declined. A declined one shows the Founders’ reason. You only ever see your own proposals, and the code of an accepted milestone only if you are allowed to see that milestone.

Deciding on proposals Founder

Founders see a Proposed section on the Plan page with a count of what is waiting (Nothing waiting. when clear), followed by the decisions of the last 30 days. Open one to read what, why, who and when, and any suggested theme, owner or date, then choose:

Accept

Tick Create a milestone for it to turn it into a real milestone: pick the Theme, edit the Milestone title, pick the Owner, and either leave it a draft or tick Agreed now (which asks for a due date and a done-when sentence and locks the date). Leave the tick off to simply note it; a note is then required. A risk with no milestone created is noted and closed.

Decline

A reason is required, and the proposer sees it.

The weekly review also lists what was proposed that week and how many are still waiting (see The Weekly Review).

“Done when” and how it is measured

Every agreed milestone and result has a Done when sentence, a plain statement of what finished looks like. Beside it is Done when · measured from, which says how Docket can check it:

  • Not measured: nobody will check it automatically; a person decides.
  • Or pick a system, then a metric from that system, then type a target such as ≥ 97% for 4 weeks.

Founders also keep a separate Target note on each item. Targets are visible only to Founders.

The ⚠ No data source chip

An amber ⚠ No data source chip on a milestone or result means no system records this today, so it will be checked by hand. It appears on a live item that:

  • is agreed but has no measure at all,
  • is set to “manual”, or
  • names a metric that no system records yet.

A draft proposal is not flagged until it has a measure. The chip is a prompt to fix the plan, not an error; the review and the re-plan list these as warnings.

The reading chip

When a metric can be read automatically, Docket checks it every morning and shows the latest reading on the row, such as 92% · 30d to 12 Nov: the value over the last 30 days. A target reached mark appears beside it when the target is met. A target that is a ceiling (“at most”) is only judged once the due date arrives, so it never shows as reached early.

Target reached: confirm done Founder

When an agreed item’s reading meets its target, a Target reached — confirm done section appears at the top of the Plan page for Founders, with a count. Each row shows the code, title, the reading, the metric and the target. Press Mark done and the normal done form opens with the evidence already filled in from the reading; edit it if you like and confirm.

✓ Docket never closes it for you

A reached target only nudges a Founder. The item stays agreed until a person confirms it is done.
