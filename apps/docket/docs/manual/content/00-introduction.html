Docket is the task manager for everyone at Legend of Toys. Whatever the work is, marketing, production, finance, a one-off errand, it becomes a task with a clear owner and a deadline, and you can follow it from the moment it is captured to the moment it is done. This manual is your self-serve guide to every screen in it.

What Docket is for

Docket answers three questions for the whole company at once: what needs doing, who is accountable for it, and is it on track. It is deliberately simple to use, type a title and press Enter and you have captured a task, and just as deliberately honest about the past: once a deadline is set it cannot be quietly edited away, and a task can never be silently deleted.

The shape of a task

Every task carries the same handful of things. You will see them again and again throughout the app.

On a taskWhat it means
OwnerThe one person accountable for getting it done. A task has exactly one owner.
CollaboratorsOther people helping. They can comment and add notes and sub-tasks, but the owner stays accountable.
TeamThe department the task belongs to, drawn live from the company's people directory.
StatusOne of five: Not started, In progress, Done, Blocked, or Abandoned.
PriorityP0 (immediate) through P3 (low). P2 is the normal default.
DeadlineWhen it is due. The first deadline you set is locked as the original promise; later changes are recorded as revisions.
PlanAn optional link to the goal (a theme, milestone or result) the task serves. Tasks with no link are called Unplanned.
IDA permanent number like DKT-1042 that never changes, so a task can always be found and quoted.
ⓘ Two principles to remember

Docket keeps an honest record. The original deadline is locked the first time it is set, every later change is logged with a reason, so “when did we promise this?” always has an answer. And tasks are never deleted; a task you decide not to do is Abandoned (with a reason), which keeps it on the record rather than erasing it.

How the work is organised

Docket organises work in two different ways, and they answer different questions:

  • A Space decides who can see a task. Work lives in your team’s space, your own Personal space, or a private space someone shared with you. Each task sits in exactly one space, and admins are not let into a space just for being admins.
  • The Plan decides why the work matters. It is the company’s list of goals (themes, milestones and results), and a task can be linked to one so that the weekly review can show how much of the week’s work moved a goal. It changes nothing about who can see a task. It sits alongside Programs, a free label for grouping tasks that belong together whether or not they serve a plan item.

On top of that, Docket counts the week as a unit: every deadline falls in a “week ending”, each team gets an on-time score for the week, and a weekly review pulls it together. Those ideas have their own part of this manual, The Plan.
