The admin Spaces screen lists every space in Docket so admins can keep an eye on the registry, and step into an orphaned private space when its owner is gone. It is the one and only sanctioned way an admin can enter a private space.

What it’s for
Overseeing spaces; rescuing orphaned ones.
Read or act?
Read, plus the audited recover action.
Who
Admin only
Shows
Space names & counts, never their tasks.

Finding it

Click Admin at the bottom of the icon rail, then Spaces in the sidebar. The sidebar lists the three admin pages (Roles & permissions, Users, Spaces), and the page is a card containing the table below.

What you can and cannot see

⛔ Metadata only, never contents

This screen shows each space's name, visibility, owner, member count and creation date, and nothing more. As an admin you can see that a private space exists and who owns it, but you cannot read a single task inside it from here. The wall holds even on the admin screen.

Reading the table

ⓘ The Founders’ space

One space is special: the Founders’ space, which holds tasks linked to founders-only plan items. Only Founders can be members. It has no Recover button, it cannot be archived, and moving a task out of it needs a Founder.

Break-glass recovery

If a private space is orphaned, its owner has left and no remaining member can manage it, an admin can recover it. The Recover button on a private space makes you its owner and a member, so the space can be managed again.

⚠ Recovery is a deliberate, logged act

Recovering a space is “breaking glass”: it is meant for genuine emergencies, not casual snooping. The action asks you to confirm, and it is permanently recorded in that space's audit trail. Use it only when there is no owner left to do the right thing, the everyday answer is for an owner to transfer ownership before they leave.

ⓘ After recovery

Once you recover a space you become its owner, so you can then re-assign it: add the right people as members and transfer ownership to whoever should run it. Recovery is a means to hand the space back, not to take it over.
