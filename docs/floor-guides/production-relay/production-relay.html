01 — How to read it

Four kinds of movement

Every arrow in this system is one of four things. Knowing which one you are looking at tells you whether you have to do something, or whether it has already happened on its own. This is the single most useful thing on the page.

Scan

A physical scan

Someone stands at a station with the scanner and scans a real label. Nothing moves in the system until the barcode is read. This is the only mechanism that proves a physical thing actually happened.

Screen

Someone clicks in an app

A human opens a screen and makes a decision — issues, approves, accepts, rejects, dispositions. If nobody opens the screen, the work waits there indefinitely.

Auto

The system does it

A consequence of the step before it. Nobody actions this — it simply appears somewhere else. Most "who was supposed to tell me?" confusion is an Auto leg that someone was waiting to be handed manually.

Handoff

Goods physically move

Material changes hands outside the system — to a vendor, to a courier, from store to floor. The system records it either side, but the middle is trolleys and trucks.

The rail colour is the baton. Each leg below has a coloured rail down its left edge showing which app owns that stretch of work. When the colour changes, responsibility has changed hands — and the yellow band at the end of each leg tells you exactly what carries it across.

04 — The unit's life

Eighteen states, and what moves between them

Every car and every remote carries one state at a time. This table is the whole state machine: what the unit is, and the exact scan or action that put it there. If you ever need to know where a unit physically is, this is the answer.

StateWhat it means physicallyPut there byStation
You cannot skip a step. Each dispatch station only accepts a unit that is already at the correct earlier stage. Dispatch In rejects anything that has not been through production's final pack; Allocate rejects a unit not yet at Dispatch In. The refusal is the system working, not a fault.

05 — First day

You just walked in. Start here.

Four entry points, one per team. Each is the shortest honest answer to "what do I actually do?" — then follow the matching leg above for the detail.

Production

"How do I start a production run?"

  1. Open Redline → New Run / Request. This is the only door. Nothing gets built without a request from here.
  2. Pick the run type: Fresh (normal build), Outsourced, Repair or Repack.
  3. Set product, line, shift, and quantities split by E-Comm and Retail.
  4. Choose the format — CKD loose parts, SKD part-built bundles, or FBU fully built cars. This is your call, not the store's, and it decides exactly what they issue you.
  5. Submit. It lands in the store's Issue Queue automatically. Now wait for parts.

Store

"What do I owe anyone right now?"

  1. Open Garage → Issue Queue. Everything production has asked for is here, colour-coded by type.
  2. Click Issue to open the pick list. Print it and go pick.
  3. Optionally scan each bag at the STORE_ISSUE station as you pick — it double-checks you against the BOM.
  4. Enter what you physically picked, tick the confirmation box, Confirm Issue. Stock comes down.
  5. Can't fulfil it? Reject with a reason. Do not improvise a substitute.

Dispatch

"What am I shipping today?"

  1. Open Depot → Overview. A red tile shows fulfilment requests waiting on you.
  2. Accept each one Full or Split. Nothing ships until you accept.
  3. On the floor, work the four stations in order: DTK → ALLOC → PACK → DOUT.
  4. At Pack, always scan the courier label too — that is the only thing that later tells us which customer got which car.
  5. Check Pipeline to see what is built but not yet shipped.

Anyone / Procurement

"I need the company to buy something."

  1. Open Snorkel → New Request. You need no vendor, no price, no part number.
  2. Write a clear title and description. Those are the two things procurement reads first.
  3. Submit. You get a PR-NNNN number and procurement takes it from there.
  4. Procurement turns good requests into a PO, which then runs the approval relay: Accept → Final Approve → Route payment → Mark Paid.
  5. Running low on a known part instead? Use Reorders — it is lighter than a full request.
